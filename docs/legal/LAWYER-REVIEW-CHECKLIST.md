# Lawyer review checklist

Prepared 2026-09-07 for the Privacy Policy v3, Terms of Service v3, Community Guidelines v2, and Disclosures v1 (sources in `docs/legal/`, published at blabberly.com/privacy, /terms, /guidelines, /disclosures). Every placeholder and every judgment call is listed here with what we assumed and why, so one hour of counsel can resolve them. Placeholders in the live pages are marked `[PLACEHOLDER: …]` and highlighted in yellow; find them all with:

```
grep -rn "PLACEHOLDER" docs/legal public/privacy public/terms public/guidelines public/disclosures
```

Facts about the product come from the mobile source (worktree `integration-2026-09-02`); code references below are relative to that repo.

## B. Judgment calls made in the documents

| # | Topic | What we wrote | Why | Alternatives |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| B1 | Minimum age | 13, with a parent-permission requirement for 13 to 17 | Matches the product: the only age control is an "I confirm I'm 13 or older" checkbox at profile creation (`src/screens/Auth/CreateProfileScreen.jsx:532`), not persisted, no birthday collected. The previous Terms said 13. | Raising to 18 would simplify alcohol content and eliminate parental-consent questions but would exclude part of the intended audience. Note the tension flagged in B2. |
| B2 | Alcohol content vs. age | Terms 10 says users must be 21 to drink, Blabberly does not verify age for bar content; Guidelines ban content promoting underage drinking | The catalog has browsable bars, breweries, cocktail tags and "Must Try Drinks" sections (`src/config/restaurantTags.js`, `src/screens/Place/PlaceDetailScreen.jsx:1169`). The App Store rating on file is 12+ (`app-store-assets.md`). | Counsel should confirm a 13+ app with browsable alcohol content is acceptable and whether the App Store rating answer for alcohol references must change (likely "Infrequent/Mild"). |
| B3 | COPPA posture | "Not for under 13; we delete if we learn of one" | Standard general-audience posture. No birthday means we do not "knowingly" collect. | An age-neutral birthday screen would make the gate defensible but adds friction and stores a birthdate. |
| B4 | California CCPA/CPRA applicability | We disclose as if CCPA applies and honor the rights for everyone, while saying Blabberly "may fall below the law's size thresholds" | Blabberly LLC almost certainly has under $25M revenue and under 100,000 consumers, so CCPA likely does not apply yet. CalOPPA does apply (any online collection of PII from Californians) and requires an effective date, categories, third parties, the change process, and a Do Not Track statement, all included. | Counsel may prefer to drop the CCPA section until thresholds are met, or keep it (our recommendation: keep, it costs nothing and reads well). |
| B5 | Sensitive personal information | Privacy 9 treats dietary restrictions that may reveal religion (halal, kosher, no pork), account login, and precise geolocation as "sensitive" and says we use them only to provide requested features | CPRA's definition includes religious beliefs and precise geolocation. Dietary picks are a proxy, not a declaration. | Alternative: do not call dietary picks sensitive. The App Store label recommendation (APP-PRIVACY-LABELS.md) is to answer "No" to Sensitive Info; the two documents take slightly different postures on purpose (conservative in the policy, industry-standard in the label). Counsel should pick one line and we will align both. |
| B6 | Public profile contents | Privacy 2 and 4 state plainly that taste preferences, dietary restrictions, notification and privacy settings, and activity counters are on the public profile record, readable without an account | True today: `firestore.rules:728` makes `users/{uid}` readable by anyone; the create/update allowlists include `tastePrefs`, `fineTune`, `notificationPrefs`, `privacySettings` (`firestore.rules:758-811`). | Product fix recommended: move `fineTune` (dietary), `notificationPrefs`, and `privacySettings` off the public document. Then simplify the disclosure. Until then the policy must say it. |
| B7 | "Saves, follows, and taste vector readable by any signed-in user" | Disclosed in Privacy 4 | `firestore.rules:1020, 1044, 1058, 911, 938, 964`. No UI exposes other users' saves, but the data is readable through the SDK. | Product fix: make saves owner-only unless a feature needs them. |
| B8 | Restaurants will see "counts, not who you are" | Privacy 5 and Disclosures 4 commit to this | Per-user rows exist in `partnerEvents` keyed by uid (`src/data/analyticsService.js`) but clients cannot read them (`firestore.rules:1609`), and the design intent is counts (`analyticsService.js:34-45`). The partner dashboard is a separate codebase. | This is a product commitment: the partner dashboard must aggregate before showing anything to a business. If the team wants to show per-user activity to restaurants, the policy must change first and consent questions arise. |
| B9 | Anthropic and user content | Privacy 5 and Disclosures 1 disclose that public caption excerpts and public route notes go to Anthropic without account information | Confirmed live code path: `scripts/enrichAboutSections.js:246-267, 487` and `scripts/enrichRouteFacets.js:152-172, 306`. A 2026-08-16 audit note called this dead code; it is not. | Confirm in the Anthropic console that the account is on commercial terms (no training on inputs). Consider excluding captions entirely to simplify. |
| B10 | Retention periods stated | 90 days raw usage events; 400 days partner counters; 90 days Sentry; 30 days server logs; "until deletion" for the rest | Code writes `expiresAt` for the first two (`src/data/engagementService.js:171`, `src/data/analyticsService.js:76`) but the Firestore TTL policies themselves are console settings and were not verifiable from code. Sentry's default retention is 90 days. Cloud Logging retention was confirmed at 30 days in the August audit. | Command center must confirm the two TTL policies exist in the Firebase console. If not, either create them or change the policy text. |
| B11 | Reports and moderation records retention | "Kept after account deletion… we are setting a fixed period and will publish it here" | No deletion mechanism exists for `reports`, `moderationFlags`, or the `moderation/` frames. Stating a period we do not enforce would be false. | Pick a period (suggest 2 years after closure, or 1 year for CSAM preservation obligations plus the case) and build the cleanup, then publish it. |
| B12 | Backups | "Deleted data can remain in database backups for a short period" | Point-in-time recovery (7 days) and daily backups are on per project notes; daily backup retention is not recorded in code. | Confirm the backup retention setting and state the number. |
| B13 | Account deletion completeness | Privacy 7 lists what is removed and what remains, including "posts where other people tagged you keep the tag" and tombstoned messages | Client sweep in `src/data/accountDeletionService.js` plus server backstop `functions/index.js` `onUserDeleted`. | B14 | Data export completeness | Privacy 8 says a few records are not in the file and offers a complete copy by email | `src/data/dataExportService.js` omits `private/contact`, `tasteEvents`, `sectionStats`, `usageDaily`, `notificationHistory`, `plans`, `collections`, `savedEvents`, `usageEvents`, `partnerEvents`. | Extend the export or keep the email path. |

## C. Facts the documents state that should be re-verified at deploy

1. Firestore TTL policies exist on `usageEvents.expiresAt` (90 days) and `partnerEvents.expiresAt` (400 days). Firebase console, Firestore, TTL tab.
2. Sentry project retention is 90 days and no PII-sending options were turned on (Settings, Security & Privacy).
3. The conversations listing rule (`firestore.rules:511-515`), plans list scoping (`:1771-1776`), and crew-only live-location rule (`:1191-1194`) that the policy relies on are the rules actually deployed to `blabbery-3010a`.
4. `MENUS_ENABLED` remains true and `PARTNER_FEATURES_ENABLED`, `BUSINESS_SURFACES_ENABLED`, and `CREW_CUT_ENABLED` remain false (`src/config/featureFlags.js`); the documents describe partner tools and paid placement as future.
5. Anthropic account is on commercial terms with no training on inputs.
6. The 43 sound-library tracks have license proofs on file.

## D. Things the documents deliberately do not claim

- That App Check, audit logging, or intrusion detection are in place (App Check is a no-op stub, `src/services/appCheck.js:36`; audit logs were off per the August audit).
- That messages can be deleted or unsent (they cannot, `firestore.rules:671`).
- That there is a private-account option (there is not, `firestore.rules:935-937`).
- That AI-generated text is labeled in the App (it is not; the Disclosures page is the disclosure).
- That moderation removes anything automatically (it does not; humans do).
- That restaurants see any user data today (they do not; partner features are off).
